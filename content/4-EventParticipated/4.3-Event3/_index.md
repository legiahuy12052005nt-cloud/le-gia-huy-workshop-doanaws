# Event Report: AWS Certification Deep Dive

### Event Objectives

- Gain a comprehensive understanding of the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02) and AI Practitioner (AIF-C01) exams.
- Understand the core concepts of AWS Cloud, including global infrastructure, high availability, and elasticity.
- Learn about the AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF), rightsizing, and security compliance to bridge the gap between being "Certified" and being "Qualified."

### Guest Speaker

The deep-dive session was led by:
- **Mr. Danh Hoang Hieu Nghi** - AWS Student Builder Group.

### Key Highlights

#### 1. Exam Blueprint: Content Domains and Weightings
- A detailed breakdown of the CLF-C02 exam structure:
  - **Domain 1:** Cloud Concepts (24%)
  - **Domain 2:** Security and Compliance (30%)
  - **Domain 3:** Cloud Technology and Services (34%)
  - **Domain 4:** Billing, Pricing, and Support (12%)

#### 2. Cloud Concepts & Global Infrastructure
- **Global Infrastructure (1.1.2):** Discussed how AWS organizes its infrastructure into Regions, Availability Zones, and edge locations. A key takeaway was the 4 criteria for choosing a Region: latency, cost, compliance/data-residency, and service availability.
- **High Availability, Elasticity, and Agility (1.1.3):** 
  - *High Availability:* Running redundant capacity across multiple AZs behind a load balancer.
  - *Elasticity:* Automatically adding and removing capacity as demand changes (auto-scaling).
  - *Agility:* The speed of deploying resources, reducing the cost of trying new ideas and decreasing time-to-market.

#### 3. AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF)
- **Understanding CAF (1.3.3):** Explored the 6 perspectives of cloud readiness (Business, people, governance, platform, security, operations).
- **Outcomes:** Reduced business risk, improved ESG performance, increased revenue, and increased operational efficiency.

#### 4. Cost Optimization & Rightsizing
- **Rightsizing (1.4.6):** The continuous process of matching resource size to actual workload needs. Using tools like AWS Compute Optimizer and AWS Cost Explorer to avoid over-provisioning and save costs.

#### 5. Security and Compliance
- Covered Domain 2 (30% of the exam), emphasizing the AWS Shared Responsibility Model, cloud security governance, and access management capabilities.

### What I Learned

#### Technical Knowledge
- Gained a clear distinction between Regions and Availability Zones, and how edge locations cache content closer to end-users.
- Deepened my understanding of architectural design choices like fault tolerance and elasticity, which turn a fixed monthly bill into a real-usage cost curve.

#### Career & Exam Mindset
- **Certified vs. Qualified:** Passing the exam makes you certified, but truly understanding the underlying principles (like why a framework exists or how rightsizing works in practice) is what makes you qualified for real-world jobs.

### Application to Work & Internships

- **Exam Preparation:** I will structure my study plan based on the 4 domains, dedicating more time to Domain 3 (Cloud Technology) and Domain 2 (Security) since they hold the most weight.
- **Applying Best Practices:** I plan to implement "rightsizing" principles in my personal AWS lab projects by constantly monitoring CloudWatch metrics to ensure I am not over-provisioning EC2 instances.

### Event Experience

Attending this Deep Dive session was a game-changer for my certification journey. 

#### Eye-Opening Lessons
- I used to think the Cloud Adoption Framework was just theoretical business jargon. However, seeing it broken down into 6 actionable perspectives helped me realize that most cloud migrations fail due to organizational reasons, not technical ones.

#### A Clearer Direction
- Mr. Hieu Nghi's presentation provided a structured, laser-focused roadmap for tackling the CLF-C02 exam. I now feel incredibly confident in my study approach and am motivated to earn my AWS Cloud Practitioner certification soon.
